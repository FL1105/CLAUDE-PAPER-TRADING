# First real-data results (2026-10-06)

Data: SII-WANGZJ/Polymarket_data, 2026-07-06 to 2026-10-05. 10,000 wallets (hash sample of the
62,839 that pass the activity filters and have at most 1,000 trades), taker side, 2.68M trades,
279k markets (95% resolved). Config: `config/backtest_90d.yaml` (42-day trailing window,
3 epochs of 14 days). Not the forward-run config.

## Walk-forward wallet selection
| epoch | eligible wallets | share of p < 0.05 (null expects 5%) | mean z | selected (BH, q = 0.10) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-17 | 275 | 5.8% | +0.09 | 0 |
| 2026-08-31 | 248 | 3.2% | 0.00 | 0 |
| 2026-09-14 | 228 | 3.8% | -0.03 | 0 |

- The p-values look like the null: no detectable wallet skill in this sample.
- The naive "top 20 by past PnL" list (what a copy-the-leaderboard approach would use) lost
  money out of sample: ROI -30%, pooled z -0.66, 37.5% of wallet-epochs positive.
- With nothing selected, the backtest placed no trades, so all outcome gates fail. Only the
  integrity gates pass. Tier: none.

## Price calibration of taker BUYs (resolved markets, before fees and spread)
Overall ROI +0.7% on 390k trades. Prices are close to calibrated in every bucket except
buys at or below 5 cents, which lost 39%. No bucket shows a stable, cost-surviving edge.

## What this does and does not show
- It does not prove no wallet has edge. It is one 90-day window, a 10k-wallet sample,
  taker side only, and short markets dominate (many wallets have few resolved markets in the
  trailing window, which limits power).
- It is evidence against "copy the top wallets by PnL" and against an easy price-bias edge.
- Next checks, cheapest first: repeat on other hash samples and the full 62,839 wallets; a
  longer window; maker side; a per-category split.

## Taker markout, all wallets (2026-09-01 to 2026-09-03, `scripts/markout_study.py`)
Value of each taker fill versus the same token's next trade at least 5 minutes later, per
dollar traded. Makers and takers are zero-sum before fees, so the negative of this is what
passive liquidity earned on average (spread captured minus adverse drift), before fees,
rebates, inventory risk and queue effects.

| entry price | fills | taker markout per $ |
|---|---|---|
| under 0.10 | 396k | -14.9% |
| 0.10 to 0.30 | 315k | -6.8% |
| 0.30 to 0.70 | 1.04M | -2.7% |
| 0.70 to 0.90 | 306k | -0.4% |
| 0.90 and up | 254k | +0.01% |
| all | 2.31M | -1.7% |

Reading: takers lose about 1.7% of notional within 5 minutes on average, and the loss is
concentrated in cheap and mid-priced tokens. That is consistent with published reports that
market making, not directional taking or wallet copying, is where the money is. It is an
average over all fills, including those a real maker would avoid or lose to informed flow.
It does not show that a retail maker can capture it: that needs quoting infrastructure, queue
position, inventory management and capital. It is a lead to test with real order books, not a
result.

## Near-resolution "bond" buys (taker BUYs at 0.80 to 0.995, sample DB)
Net of the assumed fee, returns by price and time-to-end bucket scatter around zero
(-10% to +5%, mostly within noise). Win rates sit on the implied prices. No exploitable edge.

## Independent replication: earlier window (2026-01-06 to 2026-04-06)
Same pipeline (`scripts/hf_pipeline.sh early ...`), a different 10,000-wallet hash sample, 2.57M
trades, 161k markets (98% resolved).
- BH selection again chose **zero** wallets in all 3 epochs. No trades, tier none.
- The naive top-20-by-PnL list returned **+14.0%** out of sample here (pooled z +0.37, 200 units,
  58% of wallet-epochs positive), against **-30.0%** in the recent window. The sign flips between
  windows and neither is significant: leaderboard performance is noise, not a signal.

## "BTC time windows with >65% edge" (a claim from a viral social post)
Test on 128,059 resolved "Bitcoin Up or Down" markets (5 and 15 minute, 2025-03 to 2026-10, from
`markets.parquet`). Overall Up rate 50.1%.
- By UTC hour (24 cells): best 51.5%. By hour x weekday (168 cells): best 55.9%, which does not
  survive a multiple-comparison correction (|z| 3.2 vs 3.6 needed). **No cell is near 65%.**
- Out of sample: the 10 best hour x weekday cells from the first half had an Up rate of 55.3%
  in-sample and **49.8%** in the second half (n = 3,740, z = -0.29). It does not persist.
- A weekday effect of about 1 point (49.3% to 51.3%) is statistically visible with this many
  markets but is smaller than the taker fee and spread on these markets.
Kelly sizing, volatility filters and Bayesian updating are position-sizing and bookkeeping; none
creates an edge that the underlying signal lacks.

## Wider sample: 30,000 wallets, recent 90 days (`hf_pipeline.sh wide`)
8.3M trades in the window, 455k markets (95% resolved), three epochs.
- BH selection chose **zero** wallets again in all epochs.
- Eligible wallets per epoch: 867, 759, 640. Share of p < 0.05 (null expects 5%): 4.4%, 3.7%,
  3.7%; mean z +0.03, 0.00, -0.02. Slightly under the null rate: no sign of skill, none of
  inflated tests either.
- Naive top-20-by-PnL list: ROI **-13.7%**, pooled z -1.74 (233 units), 37.5% of wallet-epochs
  positive. Three independent samples now: -30%, +14%, -14%.
- Tripling the wallet sample did not change the conclusion: no detectable copyable skill.

## Speed
Loading big trade frames with categorical ids made wallet selection about 2x faster
(22.9 s to 11.7 s per epoch on 2.6M trades) with identical output (131 eligible wallets, same
naive list). Frame loading from SQLite (about 38 s) and the backtest replay are the remaining
costs.

## Operational note
Background jobs in this cloud container stop when the session goes idle (the container is
suspended). Long unattended runs need a machine that stays up.

### Maker replay on real books (2-hour recording, 20 wide markets, back-of-queue fills)
Roughly 60 fills and $200 to $760 of volume per parameter set:
- Quoting at spreads of 0.5 to 1 cent: PnL about -$0.75 to +$0.24, i.e. about 0.0% of volume,
  plus an estimated $0.6 to $1.7 of maker rebates. Essentially break-even.
- Quoting only spreads of 2 cents or wider: 8 to 10 fills, +4.5% to +6.7% of $35 to $85 volume.
  The sample is far too small to mean anything.
- In dollar terms this is a few dollars over two hours on $25 to $100 of inventory: not an
  income, and no evidence yet that it scales.

### BTC up/down near expiry, buying the favourite at the last trade (3 more weeks)
Net ROI per dollar at 300 / 120 / 30 seconds before the end (about 2.8k markets per week):
| week | 300 s | 120 s | 30 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-04-01 | -1.8% | -0.9% | +0.1% |
| 2026-07-01 | +0.3% | +0.8% | +2.3% |
| 2026-09-01 | -1.7% | -1.1% | +0.5% |
Inconsistent across weeks and measured at the last trade price, not an ask a buyer can hit. A
real test needs the actual ask: `polybot btc5 record|analyze` records it (started this session).
A 1% edge needs on the order of ten thousand windows to detect, i.e. about five weeks of
recording on a machine that stays up.
