# First real-data results (2026-10-06)

## Morning summary (read this first)
What was tested, all on real data and all paper-only:
1. **Copying wallets** (four windows from 2025-10 to 2026-10, samples of 10k to 30k wallets,
   walk-forward, FDR-controlled): no wallet with detectable skill in any epoch; the "top by PnL"
   list is noise (-30%, +14%, -14%, +2%). Matches published studies.
2. **Price bias and near-resolution buys**: prices are close to calibrated; nothing survives fees.
3. **"BTC time windows with 65% edge"** (a viral claim): false. 128k markets, no window above
   about 56% and it does not persist out of sample.
4. **Market making**: takers lose 0.6% to 2.3% of notional within 5 minutes (a gross maker
   edge), but a conservative small passive quoter replayed on real books was about break-even
   (first 2-hour recording) and -3% to -5% of volume (second): inventory losses beat spread
   capture and rebates.
5. **BTC 5-minute favourite in the last seconds**: not tradable. The favourite has an ask only
   38% of the time at 60 s before the end and 12% at 30 s (small sample, structural cause).
6. **Fees verified** against the official docs (formula, per-category rates, makers pay nothing,
   15 to 25% rebates); config updated to version 2.

Nothing found is an income source. The wallet-copy and maker ideas are the most plausible
leads and both tested negative; the rest are closed. Not tested: information or forecasting
edge, cross-venue arbitrage, anything needing speed.

Tools built that stay useful for any strategy: fleet runner and comparison page with a
multiple-testing correction, windowed real-data pipeline, history importer with resolutions,
book/tape recorders, maker and favourite-buy replays, markout study. Faster selection (about 2x).

Operational note: background jobs stop when the cloud session goes idle; long runs need a
machine that stays up.


Data: SII-WANGZJ/Polymarket_data, 2026-07-06 to 2026-10-05. 10,000 wallets (hash sample of the
62,839 that pass the activity filters and have at most 1,000 trades), taker side, 2.68M trades,
279k markets (95% resolved). Config: `config/backtest_90d.yaml` (42-day trailing window,
3 epochs of 14 days). Not the forward-run config.

## Walk-forward wallet selection
| epoch | eligible wallets | share of p < 0.05 (null expects 5%) | mean z | selected (BH, q = 0.10) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-17 | 275 | 5.8% | +0.09 | 0 |
| 2026-08-31 | 248 | 3.2% | 0.00 | 0 |
| 2026-09-14 | 228 | 3.8% | -0.03 | 0 |

- The p-values look like the null: no detectable wallet skill in this sample.
- The naive "top 20 by past PnL" list (what a copy-the-leaderboard approach would use) lost
  money out of sample: ROI -30%, pooled z -0.66, 37.5% of wallet-epochs positive.
- With nothing selected, the backtest placed no trades, so all outcome gates fail. Only the
  integrity gates pass. Tier: none.

## Price calibration of taker BUYs (resolved markets, before fees and spread)
Overall ROI +0.7% on 390k trades. Prices are close to calibrated in every bucket except
buys at or below 5 cents, which lost 39%. No bucket shows a stable, cost-surviving edge.

## What this does and does not show
- It does not prove no wallet has edge. It is one 90-day window, a 10k-wallet sample,
  taker side only, and short markets dominate (many wallets have few resolved markets in the
  trailing window, which limits power).
- It is evidence against "copy the top wallets by PnL" and against an easy price-bias edge.
- Next checks, cheapest first: repeat on other hash samples and the full 62,839 wallets; a
  longer window; maker side; a per-category split.

## Taker markout, all wallets (2026-09-01 to 2026-09-03, `scripts/markout_study.py`)
Value of each taker fill versus the same token's next trade at least 5 minutes later, per
dollar traded. Makers and takers are zero-sum before fees, so the negative of this is what
passive liquidity earned on average (spread captured minus adverse drift), before fees,
rebates, inventory risk and queue effects.

| entry price | fills | taker markout per $ |
|---|---|---|
| under 0.10 | 396k | -14.9% |
| 0.10 to 0.30 | 315k | -6.8% |
| 0.30 to 0.70 | 1.04M | -2.7% |
| 0.70 to 0.90 | 306k | -0.4% |
| 0.90 and up | 254k | +0.01% |
| all | 2.31M | -1.7% |

Reading: takers lose about 1.7% of notional within 5 minutes on average, and the loss is
concentrated in cheap and mid-priced tokens. That is consistent with published reports that
market making, not directional taking or wallet copying, is where the money is. It is an
average over all fills, including those a real maker would avoid or lose to informed flow.
It does not show that a retail maker can capture it: that needs quoting infrastructure, queue
position, inventory management and capital. It is a lead to test with real order books, not a
result.

## Near-resolution "bond" buys (taker BUYs at 0.80 to 0.995, sample DB)
Net of the assumed fee, returns by price and time-to-end bucket scatter around zero
(-10% to +5%, mostly within noise). Win rates sit on the implied prices. No exploitable edge.

## Independent replication: earlier window (2026-01-06 to 2026-04-06)
Same pipeline (`scripts/hf_pipeline.sh early ...`), a different 10,000-wallet hash sample, 2.57M
trades, 161k markets (98% resolved).
- BH selection again chose **zero** wallets in all 3 epochs. No trades, tier none.
- The naive top-20-by-PnL list returned **+14.0%** out of sample here (pooled z +0.37, 200 units,
  58% of wallet-epochs positive), against **-30.0%** in the recent window. The sign flips between
  windows and neither is significant: leaderboard performance is noise, not a signal.

## "BTC time windows with >65% edge" (a claim from a viral social post)
Test on 128,059 resolved "Bitcoin Up or Down" markets (5 and 15 minute, 2025-03 to 2026-10, from
`markets.parquet`). Overall Up rate 50.1%.
- By UTC hour (24 cells): best 51.5%. By hour x weekday (168 cells): best 55.9%, which does not
  survive a multiple-comparison correction (|z| 3.2 vs 3.6 needed). **No cell is near 65%.**
- Out of sample: the 10 best hour x weekday cells from the first half had an Up rate of 55.3%
  in-sample and **49.8%** in the second half (n = 3,740, z = -0.29). It does not persist.
- A weekday effect of about 1 point (49.3% to 51.3%) is statistically visible with this many
  markets but is smaller than the taker fee and spread on these markets.
Kelly sizing, volatility filters and Bayesian updating are position-sizing and bookkeeping; none
creates an edge that the underlying signal lacks.

## Wider sample: 30,000 wallets, recent 90 days (`hf_pipeline.sh wide`)
8.3M trades in the window, 455k markets (95% resolved), three epochs.
- BH selection chose **zero** wallets again in all epochs.
- Eligible wallets per epoch: 867, 759, 640. Share of p < 0.05 (null expects 5%): 4.4%, 3.7%,
  3.7%; mean z +0.03, 0.00, -0.02. Slightly under the null rate: no sign of skill, none of
  inflated tests either.
- Naive top-20-by-PnL list: ROI **-13.7%**, pooled z -1.74 (233 units), 37.5% of wallet-epochs

- Tripling the wallet sample did not change the conclusion: no detectable copyable skill.

### Maker replay on real books (2-hour recording, 20 wide markets, back-of-queue fills)
Roughly 60 fills and $200 to $760 of volume per parameter set:
- Quoting at spreads of 0.5 to 1 cent: PnL about -$0.75 to +$0.24, i.e. about 0.0% of volume,
  plus an estimated $0.6 to $1.7 of maker rebates. Essentially break-even.
- Quoting only spreads of 2 cents or wider: 8 to 10 fills, +4.5% to +6.7% of $35 to $85 volume.
  The sample is far too small to mean anything.
- In dollar terms this is a few dollars over two hours on $25 to $100 of inventory: not an
  income, and no evidence yet that it scales.

### BTC up/down near expiry, buying the favourite at the last trade (3 more weeks)
Net ROI per dollar at 300 / 120 / 30 seconds before the end (about 2.8k markets per week):
| week | 300 s | 120 s | 30 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-04-01 | -1.8% | -0.9% | +0.1% |
| 2026-07-01 | +0.3% | +0.8% | +2.3% |
| 2026-09-01 | -1.7% | -1.1% | +0.5% |
Inconsistent across weeks and measured at the last trade price, not an ask a buyer can hit. A
real test needs the actual ask: `polybot btc5 record|analyze` records it (started this session).
A 1% edge needs on the order of ten thousand windows to detect, i.e. about five weeks of
recording on a machine that stays up.

### BTC 5-minute windows: the favourite has no ask near expiry (real books, 16 resolved windows)
Share of windows in which the favourite side could actually be bought, by time before the end:
240 s 100%, 180 s 94%, 120 s 88%, 90 s 69%, 60 s 38%, **30 s 12%, 15 s 0%**. Sellers pull their asks
as the outcome becomes clear (the Up token shows a bid of 0.99 and no ask). At 120 s the median
ask was already 0.93. The 16-window sample is small, but the mechanism is structural: the
"buy the favourite in the last 30 seconds" lead from the dataset study used last-trade prices
that a buyer cannot get. It is not tradable. `polybot btc5 analyze` now reports `windows_seen`
versus `windows` so the tradable share is visible as the recording grows.

### Maker replay, second 2-hour recording (20 wide markets, 14:04 UTC finish)
At quoting spreads of 0.5 to 1 cent: 70 to 80 fills, $250 to $870 volume, PnL **-3% to -4.6% of
volume** (-$8 to -$34), estimated rebates only +$1.5 to +$4.5, worst single market -$26 on
$100 inventory (price moved against held inventory). The 2-cent-and-wider setting traded 5 to 6
times (+$4 to +$5), too few to read. Together with the first recording (about break-even), the
conservative passive maker shows no positive edge on these markets: inventory losses exceed
spread capture and rebates. This is a small, short sample, and the replay assumes back-of-queue
fills, but it does not support "market making is a retail income source".

## Fourth wallet window (2025-10-06 to 2026-01-06, 10,000-wallet sample, 1.9M trades)
BH selection chose **zero** wallets again. Naive top-20-by-PnL: ROI +2.1%, pooled z -0.47 (349

## Multi-outcome arbitrage, live scan (2026-10-06, `scripts/arb_scan.py`)
500 active events scanned, 160 negative-risk events with a complete outcome set and quotes on
every outcome. Buying every YES at its best ask (or selling every YES at its best bid), net of
the documented taker fee per leg: **no basket had a positive edge** at Gamma's top of book; the
best was -0.04%, typical two-way sports sets -0.2%. "Augmented" negRisk events show ask sums as
low as 0.86 but they can gain outcomes later, so the set is not complete and it is not an
arbitrage. Consistent with the published finding that these gaps last seconds and are taken by
fast bots. Not tested: combinatorial arbitrage across related markets (needs dependency
modelling and speed).

## LEAD WORTH STUDYING: liquidity-reward farming (first look, 1.7 hours of real depth, 12 markets)
Polymarket pays daily USDC to makers whose resting orders sit near the mid (score
((v-s)/v)^2 * size, sampled each minute, two-sided counts fully). It is a documented subsidy, not a
speed contest. Tools: `scripts/reward_scan.py` (live estimate), `polybot rewards record|sim`.
- **Calm markets** (Spain PM, Iran ceasefire, Fed, Brazil election, Texas Senate; 10 of 12): a
  100-shares-per-side quote 1 cent from the mid is estimated to earn about **$8/day on about $1,000
  of capital** (about 0.8% a day), $4.75/day at 2 cents, $2.7/day at 3 cents, with **zero fills** in
  1.7 hours.
- **Fast markets** (the two Elon tweet-count markets): estimated rewards were comparable per dollar,
  but the replay filled repeatedly on one side as the price moved: -$32 of fill PnL at 1 cent
  (13 fills) against $1.1 of rewards. Informed flow picks these off. Avoid fast-moving markets.
- **What is not known:** (1) 1.7 hours says nothing about news events in calm markets, where one
  jump can fill both sides' stale quotes and lose 5 to 20% of the quoted size; (2) the reward share
  is estimated from the visible book with competitors aggregated as one maker, which could be
  wrong either way; (3) pools change daily. The only way to measure the real share is a small
  real-money test, which is the user's decision and a real risk.
- Next: record several days (`polybot rewards record --minutes 4320 --markets 12`) on a machine that
  stays up, then `polybot rewards sim --size 100` and look at calm-market net after any events.
- Context: no published APR exists; practitioners warn that one adverse event can erase weeks of
  income. At 0.8% a day the numbers are large enough that crowding must be heavy, which is the
  reason to doubt the estimate until real rewards are observed.

## Other avenues checked in the search (2026-10-08)
- **Holding rewards** (4% a year on eligible long-dated positions, discretionary): about T-bill
  yield after spreads; $1,000 earns about $40 a year. Not an income source.
- **Weather-forecast bots**: only sales copy and unverifiable leaderboards; weather data hosts are
  blocked here, so untested.
- **Kalshi-Polymarket arbitrage**: gaps are real but short-lived, need pre-funded accounts on both
  venues, and carry resolution-rule risk; hosts blocked here.
- **Sports books**: hosts blocked here; see earlier note.
